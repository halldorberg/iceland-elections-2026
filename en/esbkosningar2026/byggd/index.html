🌉Regional funds for rural Iceland

EU regional development and cohesion funds would finance roads, bridges, tunnels and infrastructure in rural areas.

The YES side argues that the EU’s regional development and cohesion funds would particularly benefit rural Iceland. It says the funds would finance roads, bridges, tunnels, and other infrastructure in rural areas.

Magnús Árni Skjöld Magnússon, chair of Evrópuhreyfingin, cites infrastructure development through regional funds as one of his three main arguments for membership. In another article, he argues that rural Iceland would especially benefit from EU membership through regional and cohesion funds and larger markets, contrary to what he calls NO-side fearmongering. He also considers rural Iceland one of the three groups that would benefit most from membership, alongside businesses and indebted households.

Thomas Möller writes on the Evrópuhreyfingin website that membership would open regional funds to Iceland, alongside the disappearance of the krona risk premium. In his view, Iceland is in a strong negotiating position in such talks.

Counter-arguments — the NO side responds directly

Iceland would pay more into EU funds than it would get back

Haraldur Ólafsson, chair of Heimssýn, says that the amounts Iceland might receive from individual funds would be only a small fraction of the contributions the country would have to pay; the EU’s cohesion and regional development funds are intended primarily for poorer states in Southern and Eastern Europe, whereas Iceland is one of the more affluent countries on the continent and would be a net contributor.

Brussels would be an even more distant authority for rural Iceland than Reykjavík

In a submitted opinion piece in Austurfrétt, it is questioned whether EU funding would improve the position of rural Iceland: it asks whether its voice would carry more weight, points out that EU rules such as the ETS emissions trading system, “thoughtlessly incorporated into Icelandic law in 2007”, raise the price of all inputs and production and reduce people’s purchasing power — remote power in Brussels would not remedy the neglect of the authorities in Reykjavík.
