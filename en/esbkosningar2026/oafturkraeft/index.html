NO-side arguments · EU referendum · 29 August 2026

🚪Leaving is nearly impossible

The experience of Brexit shows that leaving the EU is enormously difficult and costly; for a small state, membership is in practice an irreversible commitment.

The NO side refers to the experience of Brexit and says it shows that leaving the EU is enormously difficult and costly. Membership is therefore, in practice, an irreversible commitment for a small state.

Erlendur Magnússon writes on Vísir that EU membership is an almost irreversible commitment for a small state, as Brexit has shown. Haraldur Ólafsson, chair of Heimssýn, says in an article on DV that withdrawal is almost impossible and adds that no one applies for membership unless they want to join.

Hannes Hólmsteinn Gissurarson argues on Heimssýn that the EU has evolved from a customs union into a centrally governed federal state — a development which the NO side believes would be difficult to undo after accession.

Counter-arguments — the YES side responds directly

A YES vote only opens the negotiating table — nothing is settled

Foreign Minister Þorgerður Katrín Gunnarsdóttir says: “If the result is yes, the door to the negotiating table opens. Nothing else.” Nothing is settled until the process is complete “and then only if the nation itself approves the outcome” in a second referendum on a fully negotiated agreement.

visir.is · Þorgerður Katrín Gunnarsdóttir →

No legislative changes occurred during the 2009–2013 talks

Halldór Jörgen Olesen points out that no legislative changes took place during the 2009–2013 negotiation period, and that the Ministry for Foreign Affairs confirmed that no adaptation took place beyond the ordinary obligations of the EEA. A YES vote “opens negotiations. Nothing else” — the nation “keeps all its cards in hand and gets another chance to say no if the agreement is not suitable.”

visir.is · Halldór Jörgen Olesen →

Article 50 is a formal exit procedure — Brexit proves that leaving is possible

According to the information website jaedanei.is, withdrawal from the EU is formally guaranteed in Article 50 of the Treaty of Lisbon — the same process the British used for Brexit — so membership is not irreversible. Before membership could happen, in addition to a second referendum, a constitutional amendment would be required, which two parliaments would have to approve with an election in between.

A left-wing perspective: the króna is a shock absorber that accelerated the recovery after the crash, while Ireland, Spain and Greece struggled with 15–27% unemployment.
