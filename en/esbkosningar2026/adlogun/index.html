📦Talks are adaptation, not exploration

It is not possible to “just take a look”: the application process entails adapting legislation and public administration to the EU as it proceeds, and it is difficult to turn back once the process has begun.

The NO side says it is not possible to “peek inside the package”: the application process involves the ongoing adaptation of Icelandic legislation and administration to the EU. It is difficult to turn back once the process has begun.

Hjörtur J. Guðmundsson argues in an article on Vísir that the EU’s own documents show that the talks are about adapting an applicant country to the union’s regulatory framework before any vote on an agreement. In another article, he says the referendum is really about whether to begin joining the EU, since the adaptation takes place in the negotiation process itself. On BB.is, he writes that the foreign minister’s comparison with Norway’s 1994 talks does not hold because the EU’s adaptation process came later.

Kjartan Magnússon of Ung gegn ESB says that a yes vote would amount to a mandate for an extensive adaptation process to the EU’s regulatory framework and that it will be difficult to turn back. Hnikarr Bjarmi Franklínsson writes on Vísir that the process would take 3–4 years, tie up the administration, and freeze other free-trade agreements. The pace of the process is also criticized on the Heimssýn website, which argues that Iceland could enter the EU faster than any other applicant country if yes prevails.

Counter-arguments — the YES side responds directly

No adaptation took place in 2009–2013

Halldór Jörgen Olesen rejects the adaptation arguments by referring to experience: during the 2009–2013 talks, no adaptation took place — neither legislative changes nor institutional changes — as confirmed by the Ministry for Foreign Affairs in May 2026; EU IPA grants went to projects such as ecosystem mapping and translations of the regulatory framework. “A yes is not a yes to the EU. A yes is a yes to negotiate.”

No systemic changes without a second vote

Þorgerður Katrín Gunnarsdóttir says that nothing in Iceland’s system of government will be changed on the basis of the negotiations themselves; the government will sit at the table with clear negotiating objectives, and no changes will take effect until the nation has approved a completed agreement in a second referendum.

visir.is · Þorgerður Katrín Gunnarsdóttir →

A yes vote only opens the negotiation process

Páll Rafnar Þorsteinsson refers to the Venice Commission’s opinion that a yes vote leads only to the opening of the negotiation process — neither to membership nor to irreversible changes — and that the authorities must ensure that the final decision is taken in a separate vote on the agreement itself.
