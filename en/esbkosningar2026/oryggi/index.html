🛡️Security and shelter in uncertain times

In a world of tariff wars, economic sanctions and military threats, a small state needs shelter in a strong alliance; the EU protects its member states against external pressure.

The YES side says that in a world of tariff wars, economic sanctions, and military threats, small states need shelter in a strong alliance. In its view, the EU protects its member states against external pressure.

Magnús Árni Skjöld Magnússon, chair of Evrópuhreyfingin, cites security in a multinational alliance as one of his three main arguments for membership and believes that the United States’ demand for a stronger Europe and the changed security environment strengthen the case for Iceland to complete accession talks and secure shelter. Ingibjörg Sólrún Gísladóttir writes on dv.is that an isolated island nation needs allies and that accession talks are the logical continuation of Iceland’s European path since EFTA in 1970.

Páll Rafnar Þorsteinsson says on the Evrópuhreyfingin website that tariff wars and economic sanctions have become political weapons and that small states dependent on international trade need shelter in a trade alliance. Kristján Vigfússon points out that the EU responds to external threats by deepening free-trade relationships around the world and that solidarity protects small states. Vésteinn Ólason writes in Heimildin that the union is a small nation’s shelter against geopolitical threats; the EU’s main opponents in the world are Putin and Trump.

Counter-arguments — the NO side responds directly

Iceland’s special position in NATO and the defence agreement with the United States guarantee security — not the EU

Hreinn Loftsson writes on the Heimssýn website that Iceland’s special position as an unarmed nation within NATO, with a bilateral defence agreement with the United States, has ensured the country stability and peace; the EU has no military capabilities, and no member state of the Union has a comparable defence agreement. Membership could undermine the defence relationship with the United States and weaken Iceland’s special position in security affairs.

Heimssýn · Hreinn Loftsson →

Uncertainty over whether defence cooperation with the United States is compatible with the EU’s defence framework

Björn Bjarnason, former minister, says that because of the government’s plans, new questions arise about how Iceland’s bilateral defence cooperation with the United States would be compatible with Iceland becoming part of the European Union’s security and defence framework, and calls for clear answers from the authorities on whether ties with the United States would change upon accession.

bjorn.is · Björn Bjarnason →

Is the defence agreement being put at risk by EU talks?

Stefán Vagn Stefánsson, chair of the parliamentary group of Framsóknarflokkurinn, pointed out in Alþingi that Iceland’s bilateral defence agreement with the United States was a unique arrangement among NATO states and asked whether the agreement was being put at risk by EU talks.

visir.is · Stefán Vagn Stefánsson →

The shelter is a militarised great-power alliance — Iceland should stay outside the disputes of the great powers

Ágúst Valves Jóhannesson of Til vinstri við ESB says that the European Union is gradually uniting under a single foreign policy alongside defence matters; Iceland should remain a demilitarised country for the foreseeable future, free from all the disputes of the great powers, and its security does not lie in association with a larger power.
