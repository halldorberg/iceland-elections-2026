🏛️Transfer of sovereignty

Membership would transfer legislative and judicial authority to EU institutions; such a transfer would not comply with the Constitution without amendments and runs counter to the sovereignty established in 1918.

The NO side argues that EU membership would transfer legislative and judicial power from Iceland to the union’s institutions. In its view, such a transfer would not be compatible with the Icelandic constitution without amending it. It also says that the transfer would run counter to the sovereignty Iceland gained in 1918.

Jón Steinar Gunnlaugsson, former Supreme Court judge, writes on the Heimssýn website that membership would involve a sweeping transfer of sovereignty, especially legislative power, that would not comply with the constitution without amendments. Law professor Stefán Már Stefánsson argues in an article for Heimssýn that the primacy of EEA law under Protocol 35 may conflict with the constitution because it creates “super-laws” in broad areas. Erna Bjarnadóttir says at Heimssýn that the difference between the EEA and the EU is one of kind, not degree: membership would place the Court of Justice of the EU above Icelandic law.

Björn Bjarnason, former minister, says the vote is about Icelanders’ control over their own destiny and that membership would cost them control over fisheries, agriculture, and currency. Ingibjörg Isaksen, parliamentary group chair of Framsóknarflokkurinn, writes in Vikublaðið that the vote is about sovereignty and that control over fisheries and energy should remain domestic. A Reddit thread also expresses astonishment at the silence of Icelandic media about the EU’s Chat Control surveillance plans and presents privacy arguments against membership.

Counter-arguments — the YES side responds directly

Sovereignty lies in the right to negotiate

Helga Vala Helgadóttir says that sovereignty lies precisely in a state’s ability to conclude international agreements — sovereign states have always entered into such agreements — and criticizes attempts to stir up fear that Iceland’s sovereignty is at risk merely by entering into negotiations; in any case, the nation will have the final say on the agreement.

Mannlíf · Helga Vala Helgadóttir →

The EEA arrangement is unbecoming of a sovereign state

Magnús Árni Skjöld Magnússon, chair of Evrópuhreyfingin, points out that Iceland already adopts 75–80% of the EU’s internal market rules through the EEA — “rules that we have had no part in making at any stage of the process” — and says that real power over its own regulatory framework comes precisely through membership, not by maintaining the status quo.

Evrópuhreyfingin · Magnús Árni Skjöld Magnússon →

A right to shape, but no right to decide, today

Kristján Vigfússon explains that the EEA states have only a limited right to shape EU legislation at the preparatory stage, but no right to decide — at the drafting and adoption stages they have no formal involvement — and asks whether that arrangement is acceptable for a sovereign nation.

Evrópuhreyfingin · Kristján Vigfússon →

Sovereignty lies in an informed choice

Jóhannes Finnur Halldórsson writes that Iceland is already in the EEA and Schengen without full influence, and that sovereignty lies in the nation making an informed decision itself — negotiations are a natural continuation of the European cooperation already in place, not a departure from sovereignty.
