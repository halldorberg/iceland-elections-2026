NO-side arguments · EU referendum · 29 August 2026

🧭No neighbouring country is in the EU

Norway, Switzerland, Greenland and the Faroe Islands are all outside the EU and doing well; Iceland has more in common with them than with the continent.

The NO side points out that Norway, Switzerland, Greenland and the Faroe Islands all remain outside the EU and are doing well. In its view, Iceland has more in common with these neighboring nations than with the continent.

Ólafur Ragnar Grímsson, former president, sets out geographical and economic arguments against accession on Heimssýn’s website: no neighboring country is in the EU, and Iceland, Norway and Switzerland are growing faster than the EU states. On Nútímanum he adds that no Nordic neighboring nation uses the euro except Finland, and that the real-world examples do not fit the ideology of the EU.

Hannes Hólmsteinn Gissurarson argues on Heimssýn that the EU has evolved from a customs union into a centrally governed federal state, and that membership would cost, among other things, independent trade agreements. Ingibjörg Isaksen writes in Vikublaðið that EU centralization is increasing and that control over fisheries and energy should remain at home.

Counter-arguments — the YES side responds directly

Seven EU states among the top 15 — Norway’s and Switzerland’s prosperity has other explanations

Political scientist Elvar Örn Arason points out that seven EU states rank among the top 15 countries on the United Nations standard-of-living index: Denmark, Germany, Sweden, the Netherlands, Belgium, Ireland and Finland. The position of Norway and Switzerland is explained by “the exceptional resource wealth of one and the other’s special role as a financial centre — not by their being outside the EU”. Iceland and Norway base their prosperity on close ties to the internal market and are “almost associate members of the Union” without a seat at the table where the legislation is drafted.

Most of the old EFTA states joined and are well satisfied with their lot

Ingibjörg Sólrún Gísladóttir points out that “most of the states that belonged to EFTA when Iceland became a member have become full members of the EU” and are well satisfied with their situation within the Union; in a more dangerous world, it is important to have friends and allies who can come to one’s aid.

Who is in charge in the Barents Sea?

The EU has pressured the Norwegians to scale back fisheries cooperation with the Russians in the Barents Sea, but Norway is holding on to its sovereign authority as a coastal state. The conservation of the biological resources of the sea falls under the EU’s exclusive competence, and academic literature confirms that no permanent exemptions have been granted in fisheries. This explains why Norway rejected membership in 1994.
