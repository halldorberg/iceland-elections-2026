NO-side arguments · EU referendum · 29 August 2026

🤏The vanishing weight of a small state

Iceland would get about 6 seats out of 720 and ~0.08% voting weight in the Council; remote power in Brussels would be even more distant to rural communities than power in Reykjavík already is.

The NO side points out that Iceland would get about 6 seats out of 720 in the European Parliament and about 0,08% voting weight in the Council of Ministers. In its view, remote power in Brussels would be even more distant from rural areas than power in Reykjavík already is.

Hreinn Loftsson writes for Heimssýn that membership would mean only 6 seats out of 720, along with a weaker special position for Iceland in security matters. Hjörtur J. Guðmundsson says in Skessuhorn that Iceland’s weight would be about 0,08% when control rights over fishery resources were transferred to Brussels, and considers Ireland’s experience a warning.

In an article in Austurfrétt, the neglect of East Iceland by central power in the capital is linked to even more distant power in Brussels, and it is argued that ETS rules raise production costs in rural areas. Kjartan Magnússon of Ung gegn ESB says that a yes vote would amount to a mandate for an extensive adaptation process that would be difficult to reverse.

Counter-arguments — the YES side responds directly

Each Icelander’s voting weight would be 37 times that of a German

Using the Banzhaf power index, Hjálmar Vilhjálmsson calculates that Germany’s decisive voting weight in the Council of Ministers would be only just under six times that of Iceland, even though Germans are about 209 times more numerous — each Icelander would have about 37 times the weight of each German, equivalent to roughly 15 million Germans. Qualified majority voting (QMV) requires the support of at least 55% of member states and 65% of the population, which ensures weight for small states — and today Iceland’s voting weight is zero.

SJÁ / Vísir · Hjálmar Vilhjálmsson →

The smallest nations have proportionally the most weight — and are the most satisfied

Páll Rafnar Þorsteinsson points out that, relative to population, the smallest nations have many times more representatives than the largest; in addition to parliamentary seats, Iceland would receive a full seat in the Council of Ministers, a commissioner and a judge. Small states measure as the most satisfied with membership in Eurobarometer: Ireland 93%, Luxembourg and Malta 91%. Concerns about lack of influence are "entirely a home-made inferiority complex".

Evrópuhreyfingin · Páll Rafnar Þorsteinsson →

Six parliamentary seats are more than the EEA’s zero influence

Magnús Árni Skjöld Magnússon points out that Iceland already adopts 75–80% of EU internal market rules through the EEA without any voting rights; the current situation is unworthy of a sovereign state, and full membership would provide real power instead of today’s complete lack of influence.
