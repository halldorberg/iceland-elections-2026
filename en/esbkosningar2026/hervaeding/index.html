☮️Militarisation of the EU

The EU is integrating foreign and military policy and demanding increased military spending; Iceland should remain a country without armed forces and a voice for peace outside the military alliances of great powers.

The NO side says that the EU merges the foreign and military policies of its member states and requires increased military spending. In its view, Iceland should remain a country without a military and a voice for peace outside the military alliances of great powers.

Ágúst Valves Jóhannesson of Til vinstri við ESB writes that the EU is merging its foreign and military policy and that Iceland should remain a country without a military for the foreseeable future and stand with small nations. In a press release from the same organisation, Jökull Sólberg says that militarisation threatens Iceland’s role in peace and that the country should be an advocate for peace outside the EU. At the founding of the organisation, there was also a warning about the danger of Icelanders being drawn into EU militarisation.

Haraldur Ólafsson, chair of Heimssýn, mentions militarisation among his arguments against membership in an article on DV. Guðmunda G. Guðmundsdóttir writes on Vísir that EU militarisation and the adoption of its foreign policy are causes for concern.

Counter-arguments — the YES side responds directly

There is no European army — and Iceland would have a veto

Óðinn Freyr Baldursson points out that no European army exists; the idea is “the brainchild of one man” rather than EU policy. The creation of a joint military force would require the unanimous approval of all member states, so Iceland alone could block it; defence and security matters remain under the authority of each state under the Lisbon Treaty. Iceland has been without an army within NATO since 1949, without pressure to create one — why should an economic union require what the world’s largest military alliance never did?

Similar dire predictions have proved wrong before

Guðfinna Jóh. Guðmundsdóttir says claims about militarisation and loss of sovereignty are part of fearmongering against the talks; earlier dire predictions about NATO, EFTA and the EEA all proved wrong, and it is more sensible to see the agreement before passing judgment.
