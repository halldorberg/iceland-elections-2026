❓The question is misleading

The vote is in reality about an application for membership, not “talks” — the EU only accepts applications for full membership, and it is not possible to resume talks that broke off a decade ago.

The NO side argues that the ballot question is misleading: in reality, the vote is on an accession application, not “talks.” The EU only accepts applications for full membership, and talks that broke off a decade ago cannot simply be resumed.

Birgir Ármannsson, former Speaker of Alþingi, considers the question fundamentally misleading because it conflates the continuation of old talks with entirely new accession talks. The Heimssýn website states that fewer than a quarter of consultation responses supported the foreign minister’s proposal unchanged and that Carl Baudenbacher considered the question misleading because it is not possible to “continue” talks that broke off a decade ago. Erna Bjarnadóttir writes in Feykir that in 2009 Iceland formally applied for full membership under Article 49 of the treaty and that talk of “just talks” is misleading.

An opinion piece in Viðskiptablaðið says that the vote is in reality about Iceland’s membership of the EU rather than only about talks, and a submitted article in Morgunblaðið argues that the YES side avoids discussing the European Union itself even though membership is the talks’ ultimate objective. Júlíus Valsson writes at Útvarp Saga that the National Electoral Commission’s informational material conceals that a yes vote means a formal accession process. Hildur Sverrisdóttir, parliamentary group chair of Sjálfstæðisflokkurinn, says the question is misleading and the talks are a waste of time and money.

Counter-arguments — the YES side responds directly

The question is about negotiations, not accession

Foreign Minister Þorgerður Katrín Gunnarsdóttir says: “The question here is not whether the nation should join, but whether we should enter into negotiations” — a decision on membership will not be taken until the nation has seen a completed agreement and voted on it separately in a second referendum.

visir.is · Þorgerður Katrín Gunnarsdóttir →

The Venice Commission confirms the nature of the vote

Páll Rafnar Þorsteinsson points out that the Venice Commission has confirmed that a yes vote does not automatically lead to EU membership, but only to the opening of accession talks, and that the Commission reiterates that a second referendum on actual membership will be held once negotiations are concluded.

visir.is · Páll Rafnar Þorsteinsson →

Membership is not on the table

Pawel Bartoszek says the no movement is misleadingly equating negotiations with membership: “Iceland’s membership of the European Union is not on the table” — membership would require negotiations, a completed agreement, the approval of all member states, and a second referendum.
