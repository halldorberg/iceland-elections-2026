NO-side arguments · EU referendum · 29 August 2026

💸High direct cost

Membership would cost around ISK 30 billion a year in contributions, the talks themselves around ISK 2 billion, and EU climate rules have already cost Iceland tens of billions.

The NO side says that membership would cost about 30 billion krónur a year in contributions and that the negotiations themselves cost about 2 billion. It also says that EU climate rules have already cost Iceland tens of billions.

Hannes Hólmsteinn Gissurarson writes for Heimssýn that membership would cost about 30 billion annually. Haraldur Ólafsson, chair of Heimssýn, mentions in an article on DV a cost of more than 100 billion annually. Guðmunda G. Guðmundsdóttir says on Vísir that the negotiations would cost about 2 billion and the vote itself 600–700 million.

On the website Frjálst land, it is claimed that participation in the EU emissions trading system cost Iceland 144 billion in the years 2017–2024. Hnikarr Bjarmi Franklínsson writes on Vísir that the process would take 3–4 years, tie up the administration and freeze other free-trade agreements. Hildur Sverrisdóttir, parliamentary group chair of Sjálfstæðisflokkurinn, calls the negotiations a waste of time and money.

Counter-arguments — the YES side responds directly

Membership fees are outweighed by savings in the tens or hundreds of billions

Magnús Árni Skjöld Magnússon acknowledges that direct membership fees would increase, but says that extra cost would quickly be offset by savings that would "run to tens, even hundreds of billions per year" from eliminating the costs of maintaining foreign exchange reserves, tariffs and the krona’s excessive interest rates. In addition, EU regional development funds spend each year about five times more than the entire Icelandic state budget allocates to such projects.

Evrópuhreyfingin · Magnús Árni Skjöld Magnússon →

Net contribution would be closer to 2–7 billion than 30

According to the information website jaedanei.is, Iceland’s annual net contribution after accession would be around 2–7 billion krónur per year (about 0.2–0.3% of GDP based on the Finnish precedent), since the EEA contributions, about 8 billion per year, would cease — and in addition the roughly 1.4 billion in annual tariffs on seafood products to the EU would disappear within the customs union.

jaedanei.is →

The króna costs many times more than membership

Björgvin G. Sigurðsson says the cost to the national economy of an independent, floating króna is conservatively estimated at around 300 billion a year in the form of an interest-rate differential, divided equally between households, businesses and the public sector — an amount on an entirely different scale from the membership contributions under discussion.
