YES-side arguments · EU referendum · 29 August 2026

🕊️Shared European values

Membership means solidarity around peace, democracy, human rights and the rule of law with Europe’s democracies at a time of growing authoritarian forces.

In the YES side’s view, membership means solidarity for peace, democracy, human rights, and the rule of law with Europe’s democracies. It says this matters at a time of growing authoritarian forces.

Bjarki F. Guðjónsson writes on the Evrópuhreyfingin website that Iceland should join the EU to defend freedom, peace, human rights, and democracy, in solidarity with Ukraine. Jóhannes Finnur Halldórsson compares Ukraine’s struggle for Europe in Skessuhorn with Iceland’s position and says the EU strengthens small nations and protects their language and culture rather than weakening them.

Steinar Harðarson writes in Eyjafréttir that the EU is much more than an economic alliance: it combines human rights, consumer protection, workers’ rights, environmental issues, and solidarity funds. Thomas Möller argues that opponents of membership rely on an outdated image of Europe; the facts show a continent flourishing in innovation, the economy, and defence.

Counter-arguments — the NO side responds directly

A democratic deficit characterizes the EU itself

In his book on the EU, Frosti Sigurjónsson argues that a democratic deficit characterizes the Union itself; power rests with institutions in which voters have little direct say, and the Union also faces ageing populations, unfunded pension systems, and debt.

Heimssýn · Frosti Sigurjónsson →

Member of the European Parliament says the EU itself tries to influence other countries’ elections

MEP Fidias Panayiotou claims that the EU itself violates the democratic values it says it stands for: “We are trying to interfere in the elections in Moldova.” The remarks raise doubts about whether the Union lives up to its democratic promises.

Nútíminn · Fidias Panayiotou →

Peace, democracy and humanitarianism are better served on our own terms outside the EU

Jökull Sólberg and Til vinstri við ESB say that Iceland should reject EU centralization and choose international cooperation on its own terms in the interests of peace, democracy and humanitarianism; the Union’s militarization threatens Iceland’s role for peace, and the group describes itself as pro-European while nevertheless rejecting membership.
