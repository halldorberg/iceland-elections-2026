NO-side arguments · EU referendum · 29 August 2026

🗳️No mandate — broken promises

There is no electoral mandate for reviving the application; politicians promised that the EU would not be a priority issue, and a democratic deficit characterises both the process and the Union itself.

The NO side argues that there is no electoral mandate to revive the EU application. Politicians promised that the EU would not be a priority issue, and in its view a democratic deficit characterizes both the process and the union itself.

On Heimssýn’s website, Kristrún Frostadóttir is criticised for a change of course: before the election it had been “forbidden to mislead,” but now the EU issue is being pushed forward without broad consensus. Erna Bjarnadóttir writes for Heimssýn that the old membership application does not continue automatically and that there is no electoral mandate for the process. Margrét Ágústa Sigurðardóttir, managing director of Bændasamtakanna, points out in Bændablaðið that the parliamentary resolution contains no negotiating objectives and that, without an analysis of the interests at stake, the nation cannot take an informed position.

Páll Vilhjálmsson writes for Útvarp Saga that a narrow victory or low turnout would weaken the government’s mandate and that the advisory nature of the referendum opens the door to disputes over interpretation. Nútíminn quotes Member of the European Parliament Fidias Panayiotou as saying that the EU itself tries to influence elections in other countries. The NO side also believes, according to discussion on Reddit, that RÚV is biased in its coverage of the EU issue.

Counter-arguments — the YES side responds directly

The referendum itself is the search for a mandate

Helga Vala Helgadóttir responds to criticism about a lack of mandate by arguing that parliamentary elections are never about one single issue; a referendum is precisely the right tool for seeking a clear mandate from the nation on major issues between parliamentary elections — democracy is about giving the nation the opportunity to express itself now, not only once every four years.

Evrópuhreyfingin · Helga Vala Helgadóttir →

No blank cheque — the objectives are already clear

Þorgerður Katrín Gunnarsdóttir says the government is not being given a blank cheque: the negotiating objectives are set out in the opinion of the majority on the Foreign Affairs Committee, with precise conditions regarding fisheries, agriculture and energy, and the nation will have the final say on the outcome in a second vote.

visir.is · Þorgerður Katrín Gunnarsdóttir →

The negotiating objectives have been public since 18 May

Þórður Snær Júlíusson responds to claims that no negotiating objectives exist: they have been publicly available since 18. maí 2026 in the opinion of the majority on the Foreign Affairs Committee, with defined red lines on marine resources, water, energy and agriculture.

visir.is · Þórður Snær Júlíusson →

A dual democratic process

Ingibjörg Sólrún Gísladóttir, former Minister for Foreign Affairs, says the core of the issue is a dual democratic process: the nation first votes on whether talks should continue and later votes again on the agreement itself — it would be hard to find a stronger democratic mandate for the process.
