🇺🇸The Defence Agreement with the United States

Iceland’s defence is secured through the bilateral defence agreement with the United States and membership of NATO; EU membership would add nothing in that regard and could complicate, or even jeopardise, that relationship.

The NO side argues that Iceland’s security is already ensured by the 1951 bilateral defence agreement with the United States and its membership of NATO; EU membership would add nothing in that regard, as the Union has no independent military capability to defend Iceland.

Gunnar Pálsson, former ambassador, has written that participation in the EU’s security and defence policy could complicate or even put the defence relationship with the United States at risk, and international relations scholar Albert Jónsson points out that the United States will continue to defend Iceland because of its own interests, regardless of EU membership.

Counter-arguments — the YES side responds directly

Gauti B. Eggertsson: The 1951 agreement is worthless without NATO

Gauti B. Eggertsson, professor of economics at Brown University, says the 1951 defence agreement has no value if NATO falls apart — and the environment for free trade has already collapsed with Trump’s tariff war. Instead of relying on pillars that may fail, Iceland best protects its sovereignty among a group of states that respect the sovereignty of others, and there are major interests at stake in joining the EU customs union.

Davíð Þór: The defence agreement stands and falls with NATO

Davíð Þór Björgvinsson, former judge at the European Court of Human Rights, points out that the 1951 defence agreement entails that the United States defends Iceland on NATO’s behalf; if NATO were to come to an end, all the legal foundations of the agreement would collapse — “if NATO goes down the drain, the defence agreement with the United States is broken”. The United States’ unpredictable conduct means that the pillars of Icelandic security must be reassessed rather than taken for granted.

dv.is (Eyjan) · Davíð Þór Björgvinsson →

EU membership does not complicate anything — 23 of 27 EU states are in NATO

Davíð Þór Björgvinsson rejects the claim that EU membership would put defence ties with the United States at risk: 23 of the EU’s 27 member states are in NATO, the EU treaty explicitly provides that the Union’s policy shall respect member states’ commitments to NATO, and the United States itself has bilateral defence agreements with individual EU states such as Spain, Greece and Poland — EU membership and bilateral defence cooperation with the United States therefore go hand in hand in practice.

Grand Fenwick by Faxaflói

Compares the security arguments of the YES side to the novel The Mouse That Roared: seeking shelter in an alliance that has no army and has never flown a fighter jet under its own flag. Article 42 of the EU Treaty states that for NATO states, NATO remains the foundation of collective defence; Iceland has been in NATO since 1949 and has had a defence agreement with the United States since 1951, and security issues are a fallback argument because the older arguments have proved difficult to carry.
