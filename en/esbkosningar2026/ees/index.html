🤝The EEA serves Iceland well

The EEA Agreement already guarantees market access and protects interests and sovereignty better than membership; Iceland retains an independent trade policy outside the customs union.

The NO side argues that the EEA Agreement already guarantees Iceland market access and protects its interests and sovereignty better than full membership would. Outside the customs union, Iceland also retains an independent trade policy.

Erna Bjarnadóttir writes at Heimssýn that the difference between the EEA and the EU is one of kind, not degree: membership would place the Court of Justice of the EU above Icelandic law and involve a transfer of legislative and judicial power. Hannes Hólmsteinn Gissurarson argues that the EU has evolved from a customs union into a centrally governed federal state and that membership would cost, among other things, independent trade agreements. Ingibjörg Isaksen says in Vikublaðið that Iceland is doing well in the EEA.

Haraldur Ólafsson, chair of Heimssýn, rejects any further steps toward EU membership and says Iceland’s interests are best served outside the union. The website Frjálst land claims that participation in the EU Emissions Trading System cost Iceland 144 billion in the years 2017–2024 and that the country has no business being in the system. Jökull Sólberg of Til vinstri við ESB, on the other hand, says the EEA has become one-sided and that Iceland should be an advocate for peace outside the EU.

Counter-arguments — the YES side responds directly

75–80% of rules implemented without any influence

Magnús Árni Skjöld Magnússon says that Iceland already implements 75–80% of EU internal market legislation through the EEA without having had any say at any stage in drafting those rules — so the EEA only "works" by giving up the influence that full membership would provide.

Evrópuhreyfingin · Magnús Árni Skjöld Magnússon →

The formal influence of EEA states ends at the preparatory stage

Kristján Vigfússon outlines the EU legislative process in three stages and shows that the formal influence of the EEA states already ends at the preparatory stage; at the processing and adoption stages they have no involvement, and with the increased power of the European Parliament the involvement of the EEA states has "become more complex and weakened somewhat".

Evrópuhreyfingin · Kristján Vigfússon →

The EEA was a temporary solution

Björgvin G. Sigurðsson, a former minister, says that "the supplementary membership through the EEA was a temporary solution" — an excellent outcome at the time, but "not a permanent solution and democratically unacceptable in the long run"; the logical next step is to conclude the negotiations and vote on a completed agreement.

dv.is · Björgvin G. Sigurðsson →

Today the voting weight is zero

Hjálmar Vilhjálmsson, a board member of SJÁ, calculates using the Banzhaf method that each Icelander’s voting weight in the EU Council of Ministers would be about 37 times that of each German — but in the current EEA arrangement the weight of Icelanders in decisions on the rules they now implement is zero.
