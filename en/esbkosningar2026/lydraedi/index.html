YES-side arguments · EU referendum · 29 August 2026

🗳️Let the nation decide

A referendum on such a major issue strengthens democracy; voters have the right to take a position and have the final say on the agreement.

The YES side says that a referendum on such a major issue strengthens democracy. Voters have the right to take a position and have the final say on an agreement.

Helga Vala Helgadóttir writes on the Evrópuhreyfingin website that direct democracy on such a major issue strengthens democracy and that voters have the right to take a position between parliamentary elections. Ingibjörg Sólrún Gísladóttir describes the process on dv.is as a double democratic process: the nation votes on talks and later votes again on the agreement itself.

Foreign Minister Þorgerður Katrín Gunnarsdóttir emphasizes in an article on visir.is that the nation will have the final say on a completed agreement. Berglind Guðmundsdóttir, managing director of SJÁ, says that a yes means the nation gets to see the agreement and make an informed decision; SJÁ was founded at Kjarvalsstaðir with over 300 founding members.

Counter-arguments — the NO side responds directly

The question is misleading, so the vote is not an informed democratic choice

Birgir Ármannsson, former Speaker of Alþingi, considers the question fundamentally misleading: it conflates the continuation of old talks with entirely new accession negotiations, meaning voters do not know exactly what they are voting on.

Heimssýn · Birgir Ármannsson →

Baudenbacher: it is not possible to “continue” talks that broke off a decade ago

Heimssýn points out that fewer than a quarter of the submissions on the parliamentary resolution supported it unchanged, and that Carl Baudenbacher, former President of the EFTA Court, considered the question misleading because it is not possible to continue talks that broke off a decade ago.

Heimssýn · Carl Baudenbacher →

An advisory vote with low turnout provides a weak mandate

Páll Vilhjálmsson says that since the vote would only be advisory, it invites disputes over interpretation: if turnout were close to 50% and yes won by a narrow majority, the authorities would be basing the talks on the support of a minority of eligible voters — far too weak a mandate for such major changes.

Útvarp Saga · Páll Vilhjálmsson →

No is also a democratic position

Erna Bjarnadóttir says that no is a legitimate democratic position that is wrongly dismissed as hostility to information; accession talks are not a neutral fact-finding exercise, and the democratic argument of the YES side assumes that only one answer is “informed”.

visir.is · Erna Bjarnadóttir →
